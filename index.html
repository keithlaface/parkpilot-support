How it works

No account. The trip stays on the phone.

  1. Set the day

    Choose the park and the day you are going. You can import that day's plans from Calendar, and you can add people with Apple's contact and photo pickers. What you import or select stays on your device. Park Pilot has no sign-in and no backend account.

  2. Walk the park

    The app uses your location on the phone to show where you are and to calculate walking directions to your next stop. It does not send that live location to Arcem LLC or to Queue-Times.

  3. Check the line and the sky

    Wait times are requested from Queue-Times with the selected park's identifier. Weather is requested from Apple WeatherKit with that park's public coordinates. Map content comes from Apple Maps.

FAQ

Short answers. The privacy policy has the full detail on data.

Where do the wait times come from?

Park Pilot asks Queue-Times.com for waits using the identifier of the park you selected. The request does not include your live location, party, reservations, contacts, or photos. Like any internet request, Queue-Times and its network providers can see the request's IP address, the time, and ordinary client metadata.

Park Pilot shows the number Queue-Times returns. It does not operate the parks and it does not measure the queue itself. Queue-Times says its real-time data is updated about every five minutes, so a posted wait can lag the entrance. The app also reuses a wait response from memory for up to one minute so it does not send the same request twice.

What happens with no signal?

The saved trip stays on the device: the park day, plans you imported, and party details you added. Removing the app deletes that locally stored data.

Live wait times, weather, and map imagery need a connection to refresh. If a wait response was just loaded, Park Pilot can reuse it from memory for up to one minute. After that, waits do not refresh until a new response can be requested. The app does not keep a longer offline copy of wait times.

A missing signal does not upload your location. Park Pilot does not run a server that receives your trip, and it does not send your live location to Queue-Times.

Which parks are supported?

The parks you can select in Park Pilot are the supported parks. For the one you pick, the app sends that park's Queue-Times identifier for waits and that park's public coordinates for weather.

If a park is not in the app's list, Park Pilot is not offering waits or park weather for it. Open the park list in the app for the current set. Queue-Times publishes data for many more parks than any one app has to include, and Park Pilot does not claim every park on that list.

Where does the weather come from?

Current conditions come from Apple WeatherKit. The app sends the selected park's public coordinates, not your live location. Apple receives the network information needed to answer that request, including an IP address, under Apple's privacy policy.

If WeatherKit cannot be reached, weather does not refresh. The saved trip is still on the device.

Is there an account or a password?

No. Park Pilot has no backend user accounts, so there is nothing to sign in to and no password to reset. Information you enter or select is stored on your device as part of the saved trip.
